Build a Mobile App: My Student Expense Tracker

1. Project Overview

Build a fully functional, mobile-first personal expense tracker for a final-year college student living in a shared apartment in Chennai.

App name: SpendWise Student

Main goal: Help me control my spending, track every rupee, understand where my money goes, and avoid unnecessary purchases.

I live in Alamelu Apartments in the Nungambakkam/Kodambakkam area. The room accommodates six people, and my college is approximately 2 km away.

I depend mainly on outside food. I pay for rent, electricity, water, mess food, breakfast, snacks, toiletries, transport, college expenses, and other daily necessities.

The app must be simple enough to use from my phone every day. Adding an expense should take less than 30 seconds.

Important: Build a working application, not just a UI prototype. All buttons, calculations, forms, filters, and charts must work.

5. Correct Financial Calculations

This part is extremely important.

Separate actual expenses from money paid as a refundable deposit.

Use these definitions:

Actual Expenses = Sum of all non-refundable transactions.

Refundable Deposits = Sum of transactions marked refundable.

Total Cash Outflow = Actual Expenses + Refundable Deposits.

Remaining Budget = Monthly Budget − Actual Expenses for the selected month.

Category Spending = Sum of transactions belonging to that category.

Daily Spending = Sum of transactions on the selected date.

Monthly Spending = Sum of transactions in the selected month, excluding refundable deposits.

Never count the same payment twice.

A refundable house advance must appear in cash outflow and refundable deposits, but not in ordinary living expenses or monthly spending.

Allow me to select a month and view its calculations independently.

9. Reports and Charts

Create these reports using actual saved transactions:

1. Pie chart for category-wise spending.
2. Bar chart for monthly spending.
3. Line chart for spending trends.
4. Food versus non-food spending.
5. Needs versus wants.
6. Fixed versus variable expenses.
7. Top ten largest transactions.
8. Monthly budget versus actual spending.

Do not include refundable advances in charts representing ordinary spending. They may appear separately in a cash-outflow report.

Undated historical transactions should be included in overall totals but excluded from date-dependent reports until their dates are confirmed.

10. Smart Saving Features

Add a section called Save Money that helps identify:

- Categories where I spend the most.
- Repeated small purchases that accumulate over time.
- Spending on wants versus necessities.
- Remaining money available for the rest of the month.
- Categories where spending should be reduced.

Provide simple rules-based suggestions using actual recorded data. Do not display fake AI insights or invent spending patterns when insufficient data exists.

Allow me to set a daily spending limit and show how much of it remains.

11. Data Storage and Reliability

The app must preserve my information after closing and reopening it.

Use persistent local storage for the first version if a backend is not necessary.

Implement:

- Add, edit, and delete transactions.
- Automatic calculations.
- Data export as CSV.
- Full backup and restore using JSON.
- Currency formatting in Indian rupees.
- Proper handling of empty data and missing dates.
- Validation so negative or invalid amounts cannot accidentally be saved.
- Confirmation before destructive operations.

Do not require a paid API, external financial account connection, or login just to track expenses.

Do not make the app depend on a temporary session or in-memory state alone.

12. Visual Design

Create a modern, lightweight Android-friendly interface.

Use a clean white or light background, dark readable text, and restrained blue or teal accents.

Requirements:

- Responsive mobile layout.
- Large, easy-to-tap controls.
- Clear typography.
- Minimal clutter.
- Compact dashboard cards.
- Readable charts.
- Bottom navigation.
- Fast loading and smooth interactions.

The app should feel like a practical personal finance tool, not a complicated banking dashboard.

13. Final Acceptance Requirements

Before considering the app complete, verify that:

1. Every screen works.
2. Adding an expense updates the dashboard and reports.
3. Editing or deleting an expense updates every affected total.
4. The refundable advance is excluded from ordinary spending.
5. Historical totals match the provided transaction list.
6. Unknown dates are not fabricated.
7. Budgets can be edited without changing transaction history.
8. Data persists after the app is closed.
9. CSV export and JSON backup work.
10. The layout works properly on a mobile screen.

Build the complete working app now. Prioritize correct calculations, reliable data storage, and fast daily expense entry over unnecessary animations or complicated features.
